TASK. Write, from scratch, a RASP program that Tracr compiles into a policy network that plays 9 x 9 Go as strongly as possible. The network's weights are exactly what the compiler produces from your program; you do not set them yourself. The goal is to win as many games as possible against the opponents described under HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED.

THE GAME: Go on a 9 x 9 board (the pgx implementation, "go_9x9").
Rows are numbered 0 (top) to 8 (bottom), columns 0 (left) to 8 (right). Black moves first; players alternate. A move either places a stone of the mover's colour on an empty point or passes. A group of stones with no adjacent empty point is captured and removed. Suicide is illegal, and so is retaking a single stone immediately (simple ko). A move that repeats an earlier whole-board position ends the game at once, and the player who made it loses. The game ends after two consecutive passes, or after 162 moves in total. Then it is scored by area: each player's stones on the board plus the empty points surrounded only by that player's stones; White receives a komi of 7.5 points. The higher score wins (no draws).

THE NETWORK. Your network is a transformer compiled by Tracr (DeepMind's compiler from RASP programs to transformer weights; the version installed in your sandbox, whose source you may read) from a RASP program that you write. The file /work/program.py must define a function program() that builds and returns the output SOp (it may import your other modules in /work); it may also set MLP_EXACTNESS (an integer, default 100). The network is
  compiling.compile_rasp_to_model(program(), vocab={".", "x", "o", "B", "W"}, max_seq_len=82, compiler_bos="BOS", mlp_exactness=MLP_EXACTNESS)
(from tracr.compiler import compiling; causal = False), used exactly as Tracr produces it: you choose the program, not the weights.
Input: the compiled network reads 83 tokens: "BOS", then the 81 points in the order row * 9 + column (in your program, rasp.indices of the point at row r, column c is r * 9 + c), from the point of view of the player to move: "x" = a stone of the player to move, "o" = a stone of the opponent, "." = empty; then, at index 81, "B" if Black is to move or "W" if White is to move.
Output: program() must return a numerical SOp (rasp.numerical); its value at index i = 0 .. 81 in the compiled network is the logit of move i: i = row * 9 + column plays at that point, i = 81 passes.
The compiled network is run with Tracr's own forward pass (tracr/transformer/model.py): x = token embedding + position embedding (83 x D); then for each layer x = x + MultiHeadAttention(x), with attention weights softmax((x W_Q)(x W_K)^T / sqrt(key_size)) over all 83 positions, and x = x + relu(x W_1 + b_1) W_2 + b_2; no layer norm. The logit of move i is x[i + 1, d], where d is the residual dimension of your output SOp. Tracr's compilation is not guaranteed to be exact, so the compiled network's outputs can differ from what rasp.evaluate gives for your program: what plays is the compiled network.
Illegal moves (occupied points, suicide, simple ko) are removed before every move, and the move is sampled from the softmax of the remaining logits (temperature 1). There is no search: the network's output is the whole policy.
Limits: compiling /work/program.py must succeed within 900 seconds and 10 GB (the limits of a sandbox run), and the compiled network must take at most 60 seconds per 1,000 positions on our machine (4 CPU cores, our NumPy implementation of the forward pass above, which matches Tracr's to about 1e-5). compile_program() (below) reports both.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, your network plays 2,000 games against each of several hidden opponents: policy networks for this game (among them two trained with reinforcement learning by self-play, which beat a uniform random player 97-98 % of the time), and a uniform random player. Your network is Black in half of the games; the moves of both players are sampled from their policies; there is no search. Score: wins / games; the main measure is the average over the two networks trained with reinforcement learning. You never see these opponents.

RULES. Not allowed: (a) gradient descent or any other training: no backward passes, autograd or optimizers, and no fitting of numbers in your program to data or labels (no regression or least squares on examples); (b) search: no game-tree search (MCTS, minimax, rollouts, looking ahead over sequences of moves) in the sandbox, neither to choose moves nor to produce target moves or values for designing your program; (c) running the network outside the pass tools: do not evaluate your RASP program (rasp.evaluate, calling an SOp on an input, or an evaluator of your own) or the compiled network (Tracr's apply or forward, or a forward pass of your own over its weights) on any input in the sandbox; (d) changing the weights: the network is the compiler's output as it is; (e) internet access (there is none). Allowed: writing the RASP program from your own design, by hand or generated by code (formulas, loops, tables you work out); reading Tracr's source; compiling your program and inspecting the compiled model's structure and weights; Go-rule code in the sandbox (for example to work out the liberties of groups on boards you choose) as long as it does not run your program or the network or search; and any analysis of the pass results.
